# Assignment requirements

Source: `CTS2000 Course Outline(1).pdf`, supplied by the group, pages 4–8.
This is a working transcription of the requirements, not a replacement
for the original brief or later CourseLink announcements.

| Deliverable | Weight | What the outline requires | Due |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Topic statement | 10% | 2 pages; motivation, question(s), dataset and relevance; members and equitable division of labour outside page count | Oct. 9, 2026, 5 p.m. |
| Research paper | 20% | 5–7 pages; question, context, data, methods, supported answer, visualization, limitations and future research | Dec. 7, 2026, 5 p.m. |
| Repository | 10% | GitHub used regularly for original code, careful annotations, experiments, intermediate results and identifiable individual contributions | Dec. 7, 2026, 5 p.m. |
| Presentation | 10% | One Jupyter Notebook, run live; 10-minute talk + 10-minute Q&A | Final week; exact group slot TBD |

Times use America/Toronto. The outline lists presentation sessions on
Nov. 30, Dec. 2 and Dec. 4. Group check-ins are listed for Weeks 9–11;
Week 11 is Nov. 23 / Nov. 25. No exact check-in cutoff is given here.

## Research paper checklist

- [ ] State one concrete question and situate it in secondary scholarship.
- [ ] Describe the dataset, selection and preparation.
- [ ] Explain the methods and why they answer the question.
- [ ] Support the answer with evidence from the analysis.
- [ ] Include at least one data visualization and explain its significance.
- [ ] Discuss limitations, unsuccessful methods and missing data.
- [ ] Suggest future research directions.
- [ ] Edit the paper into a coherent shared voice.
- [ ] Add a bibliography and an author contribution statement.

Bibliography and author contributions are outside the stated page count.
Each student writes one or two sentences about their real contributions.

## Presentation checklist

The final presentation notebook must contain three sections:

1. Dataset and research question.
2. Methods.
3. Answer supported by analysis and visualization.

- [ ] Test the notebook live with the source data.
- [ ] Rehearse a 10-minute talk.
- [ ] Prepare for 10 minutes of questions.
- [ ] Confirm the group assigned to us for kickstarting its Q&A.

## Collaboration and process records

Each member should contribute substantively. The outline explicitly says
division of labour should not simply mean one person codes while another
person writes. Record actual work in commits, pull requests, check-ins
and contribution statements. Creating this scaffold does not establish
anyone's completed contribution or reconstruct earlier GitHub history.

Formal assignments normally use CourseLink DropBox; a repository or pull
request does not by itself establish that a submission was made.

## AI records required by the outline

Record tool/model version, date/time, prompts and output; retain recoverable
versions and distinguish AI assistance from the group's own contribution.
Use [ai_usage.md](ai_usage.md) and add the missing earlier records. This is
documentation work, not a requirement to ask permission for routine edits.
